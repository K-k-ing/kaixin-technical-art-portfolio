01 / Research question

Visually convincing does not mean spatially reliable.

World models can explain what something is, where it is, and what it relates to. A semantically plausible scene does not necessarily have reliable depth, dimensions, cameras, or final production geometry.

I first tested Astra's scene understanding and layout descriptions, then compiled structured results into editable Unreal Actors and used object-level SpatialPatch operations to adjust local layouts.

Single-view ambiguity and spatial drift led me to separate model suggestions, reconstruction evidence, human-accepted scene decisions, and program execution. The model proposes candidates; it does not make every decision for the final scene.

Marble's Gaussian / PLY reconstructions provide visual and spatial clues. I experiment with camera projection, registration, and queries in source coordinates, without treating the reconstruction as a reliable production asset.

03 / Bounded floor instancing case · Implemented

One mesh. Nine placements.
Not nine observed facts.

In a bounded floor case, I recorded six placements observed in the source data and three placements inferred through design, then compiled them into Blender with all nine sharing the same mesh data.

6 observed placements

Retain the evidence source

Record placements supported by source data, without allowing later visual additions to overwrite the original facts.

3 design-inferred placements

Acknowledge the design choice

Keep a separate source for each inference and make clear that it does not carry the authority of geometric ground truth.

1 shared mesh

Execute decisions deterministically

Reuse production geometry in this bounded case instead of generating a separate model for every placement.

This result demonstrates decision recording and instancing compilation in the floor case. It does not establish a completed general Scene Graph, full Asset Resolver, or automatic generation of arbitrary scenes.

04 / Asset fitting & reconstruction diagnosis · Experimental

Reconstruction is not production geometry.

A good-looking candidate
may still not fit.

I tested placing an externally generated chair in the reconstructed space, inspecting camera projection, position, size, and shape. Asset appearance and a candidate mesh do not directly determine its position in world space.

Local depth and backrest errors remained after fitting, requiring human acceptance thresholds. One chair experiment cannot establish a general, automatically successful asset-replacement service.

01 / Scene crop & standalone reference

First, identify the object to replace.

Starting with the chair in the scene, I prepared references for comparing shape. The standalone reference adds appearance information, but does not directly supply real dimensions, rear geometry, or world coordinates.

02 / Fragmented reconstruction & candidate multi-view

The reconstruction provides clues.
The candidate provides different geometry.

I compare the fragmented reconstruction with the generated asset to inspect what each retains and what each lacks. These are not before-and-after repairs of the same mesh, and a complete-looking shape alone does not make the candidate a suitable replacement.

03 / Part-color structure inspection

Beyond looking like a chair:
inspect how its parts relate.

Colors help me inspect the extent and connections of different parts, without assuming that a similar overall silhouette means every local structure is correct.

This inspection image does not validate automatic semantic segmentation, prove that parts are separately editable, or establish production-ready topology.

05 / Appearance candidate & scene review

Return to the scene,
not just the isolated asset.

I first inspect the candidate's material appearance, then return to the reconstructed environment to observe its visual relationship to the tabletop, floor, and nearby objects. A complete-looking asset does not mean its world-space position, scale, or occlusion has passed acceptance.

WorldSculpt: diagnosing fragmented output and continuous surfaces.

The early GLB loaded normally but consisted of many disconnected fragments. After investigating a weight-key mismatch and loading checks, the repair experiment produced a more continuous surface, with thin sheets, cracks, and residual fragments still present.

WorldSculpt is an archived, focused experimental branch, not part of the default workflow. Dense reconstruction can provide geometric evidence, but computational cost, thin structures, topology, UVs, and production materials still limit its direct use in real-time asset production.

05 / Current results & capability boundaries

Make progress clear.
Keep the unknowns visible.

Implemented
Structured Unreal Actor compilation and local SpatialPatch operations; observation / inference records and shared-mesh instancing in the bounded floor case.
Experimental
Astra semantic and layout baselines, Gaussian / PLY queries, camera registration, external-asset fitting, and the archived WorldSculpt repair experiment.
In development
A general Scene Graph and integration between the independent experimental stages.
Proposed
A general Asset Resolver, automated UV / PBR production processing, and a complete Blender-to-Unreal delivery and acceptance loop.
Future direction
Explore interactive worlds that continue to generate as they are observed, and a continuous workflow from generated observations to structured scenes.

WORLDIR × MATE / Future integration direction

WORLDIR organizes scene requirements.
MATE supports asset and material iteration.

WORLDIR studies spatial evidence and structured decisions at scene level. MATE studies review, binding, and editable surface iteration at asset level. They are currently independent prototypes; automatic requirements handoff and return to the scene remain future directions, not a working end-to-end system.
